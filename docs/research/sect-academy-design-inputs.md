# Pay for recall, gate with exams

Build the scheduler on **FSRS-6 with its published 21 default weights, desired retention 0.9 and no optimizer**; cap the daily load at roughly **8-10 new items and a 15-20 minute session**; default the security track to the **new Security+ SY0-801** and the **September 2026 ISC2 CC outline**; and make the sect economy pay only for scheduled recall while every large gate is a real test. FSRS-6 is the version shipped in Anki and in the stable Python reference library, it has closed-form interval maths that fit an offline, dependency-free implementation, and in the last benchmark that included SM-2 the FSRS family had about half the log loss. The language evidence points the same way as the scheduler: keep testing items after the first correct answer (about 80% versus 33-36% recall a week later), prefer typed recall, and interleave grammar contrasts across sessions. The certification landscape moved under most existing study material in 2026: Security+ SY0-801 launched on 17 November 2026 with new weights and an AI-threats objective, the ISC2 CC got a new outline, adaptive testing and a US$199 fee, and Network+ N10-009 is unchanged but near the end of its life. On the game side, the published product data says a tiny daily unit plus forgiveness retains better than ambitious goals, and the motivation literature says rewards must read as feedback about competence, not payment for volume. Three caveats matter: the "20-30% fewer reviews" claim for FSRS is unverified, most payout numbers below are proposals to playtest, and the Spanish A1 inventory rests on a university syllabus because the official Instituto Cervantes source was unreachable.

## FSRS-6 with default weights beats SM-2 on calibration, not provably on workload

### Why FSRS-6 and not SM-2 or FSRS-7

**FSRS-6 is the current stable, deployed version**: py-fsrs `main` is 6.3.3 with a 21-value default parameter tuple, and Anki 26.09.4 pins the FSRS-6 crate ([py-fsrs scheduler.py](https://github.com/open-spaced-repetition/py-fsrs/blob/main/fsrs/scheduler.py), [anki Cargo.toml](https://github.com/ankitects/anki/blob/main/Cargo.toml)). FSRS-7 exists in fsrs-rs 7.0.0, but it stores two stabilities, needs 34 parameters, and loses the analytic interval: "intervals are calculated iteratively" ([awesome-fsrs wiki](https://github.com/open-spaced-repetition/awesome-fsrs/wiki/The-Algorithm)). Its two published default vectors also disagree (34 values in the wiki, 35 in the benchmark README), so neither should be copied into code ([srs-benchmark README](https://github.com/open-spaced-repetition/srs-benchmark/blob/main/README.md)). Store the weight vector as data per scheduler so a later move is a function swap, not a schema change.

The accuracy case against SM-2 is strong. In the September 2024 benchmark (19,990 users, about 703 million reviews), **SM-2 scored a log loss of 0.71 against 0.346 for trained FSRS-5 and 0.373 for FSRS-5 on default parameters**, and FSRS-5 had lower calibration error than SM-2 for 99.0% of users ([srs-benchmark README @ 8a75a35](https://github.com/open-spaced-repetition/srs-benchmark/blob/8a75a35/README.md)). FSRS-6 beats Anki's SM-2 variant on log loss for 99.6% of users, with the stated caveat that no fully fair comparison exists because SM-2 was never designed to predict probabilities ([Expertium](https://expertium.github.io/Benchmark.html)). The default-parameter row is the one that matters here, since this app will ship without an optimizer; no FSRS-6 default-parameter row is published, so "FSRS-6 defaults beat SM-2" is an extrapolation from the FSRS-5 and FSRS-7 default rows.

The workload case is weak, and the report should not oversell it. The FSRS wiki itself now flags the "20-30% fewer reviews" figure as "based on very old simulation results, take it with a grain of salt" ([ABC of FSRS](https://github.com/open-spaced-repetition/awesome-fsrs/wiki/ABC-of-FSRS)). No controlled study comparing FSRS and SM-2 on measured retention and review counts was found. The practical product advantage is different: FSRS gives one "desired retention" knob and handles late reviews gracefully, because stability after a delayed success "converges to an upper limit" instead of growing linearly as in SM-2 ([awesome-fsrs wiki](https://github.com/open-spaced-repetition/awesome-fsrs/wiki/The-Algorithm)). For reference, SM-2 is `I(1)=1`, `I(2)=6`, `I(n)=I(n-1)*EF`, with EF starting at 2.5, floored at 1.3, and updated by `EF' = EF + (0.1 - (5-q)*(0.08 + (5-q)*0.02))` ([SuperMemo](https://super-memory.com/english/ol/sm2.htm)). One limitation carries over: FSRS-6 is no better than a constant at predicting same-day recall (0.3842 log loss versus 0.3816 for the average baseline), so in-session re-shows are a product decision, not an algorithm output ([srs-benchmark README](https://github.com/open-spaced-repetition/srs-benchmark/blob/main/README.md)).

### Exact formulas and defaults

The default weight vector, identical in the wiki and py-fsrs, is `[0.212, 1.2931, 2.3065, 8.2956, 6.4133, 0.8334, 3.0194, 0.001, 1.8722, 0.1666, 0.796, 1.4835, 0.0614, 0.2629, 1.6483, 0.6014, 1.8729, 0.5425, 0.0912, 0.0658, 0.1542]` ([py-fsrs scheduler.py](https://github.com/open-spaced-repetition/py-fsrs/blob/main/fsrs/scheduler.py)). All formulas below are FSRS-6 as implemented there, with grade G in 1-4 (Again, Hard, Good, Easy), t in whole days, S clamped to at least 0.001 and D clamped to [1, 10].

| Quantity | Formula |
|---|---|
| Constants | `DECAY = -w20`; `FACTOR = 0.9^(1/DECAY) - 1` |
| Retrievability | `R(t,S) = (1 + FACTOR * t / S) ^ DECAY` |
| Initial stability | `S0(G) = w[G-1]` |
| Initial difficulty | `D0(G) = w4 - e^(w5*(G-1)) + 1` |
| Difficulty update | `D' = D - w6*(G-3)*(10-D)/9`, then `D'' = w7*D0(4) + (1-w7)*D'` (unclamped D0(4)) |
| Stability after recall (G >= 2) | `S' = S * (1 + e^w8 * (11-D) * S^(-w9) * (e^((1-R)*w10) - 1) * hard * easy)`, `hard = w15` if G=2, `easy = w16` if G=4, else 1 |
| Stability after lapse (G = 1) | `S_f = w11 * D^(-w12) * ((S+1)^w13 - 1) * e^((1-R)*w14)`, then `min(S_f, S / e^(w17*w18))` |
| Same-day review (< 1 day) | `S' = S * e^(w17*(G-3+w18)) * S^(-w19)`, with the multiplier floored at 1 when G >= 2 |
| Next interval | `I = (S / FACTOR) * (r^(1/DECAY) - 1)`, rounded, min 1 day, max `maximum_interval` |

Order of operations matters for matching the reference: R is computed from the old S, the new S uses the old D, and D updates on every review after the first. The reference defaults are **desired retention 0.9, learning steps of 1 and 10 minutes, one 10-minute relearning step, a 36,500-day maximum interval and fuzz enabled**; Anki's defaults match ([py-fsrs scheduler.py](https://github.com/open-spaced-repetition/py-fsrs/blob/main/fsrs/scheduler.py), [anki deckconfig](https://github.com/ankitects/anki/blob/main/rslib/src/deckconfig/mod.rs)). Anki's manual says 90% "offers a good balance of retention and workload" and that above 90% "the workload increases very quickly" ([Anki manual](https://docs.ankiweb.net/deck-options.html)). The research notes include a roughly 55-line pure-Python port that the researcher reports matched py-fsrs 6.3.3 with zero mismatches across 75,000 simulated reviews with fuzz off; that port matches py-fsrs, which its own README warns can differ slightly from Anki ([py-fsrs README](https://github.com/open-spaced-repetition/py-fsrs/blob/main/README.md)). The researcher's derived values from the defaults, useful as unit-test fixtures, are first intervals of 1, 1, 2 and 8 days for Again, Hard, Good and Easy, and a Good-only sequence of 2, 11, 46, 163 and 497 days.

### Grading, steps and review debt

Map **wrong to Again (1) and right to Good (3)**. The maintainers say Again/Good-only use works and is "a little more accurate", and the one habit FSRS cannot absorb is recording a failed recall as Hard, because Hard is a passing grade and "all intervals will be unreasonably high" ([fsrs4anki tutorial](https://github.com/open-spaced-repetition/fsrs4anki/blob/main/docs/tutorial.md), [Anki manual](https://docs.ankiweb.net/deck-options.html)). Store the grade as 1-4 anyway. Never auto-assign Easy from answer speed, and collapse any LLM-graded free text to pass or fail before it reaches the scheduler; both are design recommendations, not sourced rules. For steps, FSRS guidance is that all learning steps finish the same day and that many short steps are discouraged ([fsrs4anki tutorial](https://github.com/open-spaced-repetition/fsrs4anki/blob/main/docs/tutorial.md)). The simplest faithful design for a session-based app is a single step implemented as "re-queue the missed card later in this session".

Review debt is the main way a beginner app fails. Anki's defaults are 20 new and 200 reviews a day, tied by an explicit rule that the new-card limit "should typically be at least 10x smaller than your review limit", and by default no new cards appear once the review limit is hit ([anki deck-config.ftl](https://github.com/ankitects/anki/blob/main/ftl/core/deck-config.ftl)). The recommended default for this app is **about 10 new items a day with a review cap of ten times that, reviews served before new items, and new items throttled automatically while a backlog exists**. That number is a derivation from the 10x rule, not a sourced beginner figure. Keep a review log (card, grade, timestamp) from day one, since it is the input any future optimizer needs, and consider disabling fuzz for small decks so tests are deterministic.

## A1 is about 600-700 words and 12 units, learned by typed recall

### Scope: French needs a past tense, Spanish does not

CEFR A1 means the learner "can understand and use familiar everyday expressions and very basic phrases aimed at the satisfaction of needs of a concrete type" ([Council of Europe](https://www.coe.int/en/web/common-european-framework-reference-languages/table-1-cefr-3.3-common-reference-levels-global-scale)). In both languages that resolves to the present tense, articles with gender and number agreement, basic questions and negation, numbers and time, and personal-information functions. The two inventories diverge in one place that affects course length. **French A1 includes a limited passé composé** (with avoir, and with être for a few verbs), plus futur proche, passé récent, partitive articles, three question forms and ne... pas / ne... jamais; futur simple, imparfait, object pronouns and relative pronouns sit at A2 ([digiSchool, from the FEI inventory](https://www.digischool.fr/articles/fle/grammaire-delf-a1-a2-b1-b2/)). **The Spanish A1 programme read for this research lists only the present indicative**, alongside the ser/estar/hay contrasts, gustar-type verbs, usted forms, direct-object pronouns and para + infinitive ([UCM A1 grammar programme](https://www.ucm.es/data/cont/media/www/pag-41464/a1_gramatica.pdf)).

Two sourcing limits apply. The Spanish list comes from a Complutense syllabus built on the Plan Curricular del Instituto Cervantes, not from the Plan itself, and the French list is second-hand from a site summarising the official inventory. The proposed structure is twelve function-first units per language, moving from survival phrases and "who I am" through describing, daily actions, asking, time, places, food and shopping to plans, with French ending on a "what happened" unit for the passé composé. That ordering is a design synthesis, not a syllabus. Including Spanish `ir a + infinitive` is likewise a design choice, since it is absent from the UCM list. The Spanish pack must also pick and declare a variety (es-ES or es-MX), and both packs deserve a native-speaker review, because the list of classic pain points for English speakers was not sourced.

### Vocabulary: front-load verbs and function words

Frequency data justifies a small, sharply chosen word list. In Davies' Spanish corpus, **the first 1,000 lemmas cover 76.0% of non-fiction, 79.6% of fiction and 87.8% of speech**, and the second thousand adds only 4.9 to 8.0 points ([Davies](https://www.mark-davies.org/articles/davies_26.pdf)). Verbs concentrate far more than nouns: 231 verb lemmas cover 90.6% of spoken verb tokens, while it takes 1,289 nouns to cover 79.3% of noun tokens ([Davies, Table 5](https://www.mark-davies.org/articles/davies_26.pdf)). The design consequence is to teach function words and the top 30-50 verbs thoroughly, then choose nouns by A1 situation. A target of **600-700 lemmas per language, about 50-60 per unit**, is a defensible design decision, but no authoritative source fixes A1 at that size, and equivalent coverage bands for French were not found.

### Exercise formats: keep testing, type the answer, mix across days

The strongest single result for this app is Karpicke and Roediger's Swahili study: repeated testing gave about 80% recall after a week, while dropping items from testing after one correct answer gave 36% and 33%, and "repeated study after one successful recall did not produce any measurable learning a week later" ([Karpicke & Roediger 2008](https://www.brucehayes.org/Teaching/papers/2008_Roediger_Karpicke_Science.pdf)). The implication is that **no item retires after one correct answer**, which is exactly what a spaced scheduler enforces. Interleaving has a narrower payoff than is often claimed. In four experiments on Spanish preterite versus imperfect, interleaving gave no advantage within a single session, but across two weekly sessions it gave "substantially better performance on a one-week delayed test" ([Pan et al. 2019](https://sc-pan.github.io/pdf/PTLOR_2019W.pdf)). So introduce each new point in a short blocked burst, then mix contrasts (ser/estar, le/la, tu/vous) in later review sessions.

The proposed format ladder per item is first exposure with audio and an example sentence, then multiple choice as warm-up only, then typed L2-to-L1, typed L1-to-L2, sentence cloze and listen-and-type, promoting on success and demoting on failure. Multiple choice should never grant mastery. This ladder is a design proposal; the supporting evidence on recall-versus-recognition formats was seen only in summary, and evidence on audio, sentence context and cloze was not sourced.

### Session size and the honest time-to-A1 promise

Institutional estimates put A1 at **60-100 guided hours** ([Alliance Française Brussels](https://alliancefr.be/en/learn-french/course-structure)), with Cambridge quoting about 90-100 hours as a guideline ([Cambridge English](https://support.cambridgeenglish.org/hc/en-gb/articles/202838506-Guided-learning-hours)). At 80 hours, 20 minutes a day takes 240 days, so the honest promise is roughly six to ten months, and self-study without a speaking partner may run longer. Applying Anki's 10x rule, 8 new items a day implies about 80 daily reviews, which at 6-8 seconds each is 8-11 minutes and leaves room for new material in a 15-20 minute session. No research on optimal session length was found, so the session shape is a proposal: due reviews in mixed formats (capped at about 60-80), one small new chunk of 5-8 words plus one grammar point, a short applied task such as a 20-40 word typed message, and a preview of tomorrow.

One offline constraint shapes the audio design. `speechSynthesis` is widely available, but Chrome desktop's Google voices "require online access with no offline fallback", and Android can list a language with no installed voice pack and fall back to an English voice ([Readium](https://readium.org/speech/docs/WebSpeech.html), [MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/SpeechSynthesis)). French read by an English voice teaches wrong pronunciation, so the app should filter voices by language at runtime, prefer local voices, hide the audio button when no fr or es voice exists, and treat bundled audio files as the reliable offline option.

It adds objective 2.6 on AI usage (poisoning, prompt injection, jailbreaking, hallucinations, model manipulation), moves mitigations into a consolidated objective 4.1, and lists incident response as preparation, identification, investigation, containment, negotiation, eradication/recovery, notification and post-incident ([SY0-801 Exam Objectives](https://lecbyo.files.cmp.optimizely.com/download/77f3bd3223ac11f180820e495f189928)). The app should default to SY0-801, keep SY0-701 as a legacy track that expires on 11 June 2027, and weight a 90-question mock at roughly 14 / 22 / 17 / 24 / 13. The 750 pass mark is a scaled score, not a percentage ([Destination Certification](https://destcert.com/resources/security-plus-passing-score-2/)). Price was not confirmed on a vendor page; third parties report US$439 after a June 2026 increase ([Training Camp](https://trainingcamp.com/articles/how-to-save-money-on-comptia-exam-vouchers-a-practical-guide/)).

### Network+: stable, but store the exam code as data

N10-009 weights are **Networking Concepts 23%, Network Implementation 20%, Network Operations 19%, Network Security 14% and Network Troubleshooting 24%** ([CompTIA Network+](https://www.comptia.org/en-us/certifications/network/)). CompTIA shows no successor and estimates retirement in 2027, so an N10-010 announcement during the app's life is likely. Troubleshooting is the largest domain and is scenario-heavy, which argues for "symptom, most likely cause, next step" items over definitions. A 90-question mock weights to about 21 / 18 / 17 / 13 / 21.

The September 2026 outline is ISC2's first major content update since the 2022 launch and folds foundational AI topics throughout ([ISC2 Insights](https://www.isc2.org/insights/2026/08/inside-the-updated-isc2-cc-exam)).

A fixed 100-item, 120-minute mock with back-navigation disabled is the closest practical approximation of the adaptive exam.

### What this means for the question bank

Terminology is exam-specific, so a question correct for one exam can be wrong for another: CompTIA tests four control categories and six types, while ISC2 CC tests three categories ([ISC2 CC Exam Outline](https://www.isc2.org/certifications/cc/cc-certification-exam-outline)). Incident-response ordering is the highest staleness risk, because SY0-701, SY0-801 and NIST all differ, and NIST SP 800-61 Rev. 3 superseded Rev. 2 in April 2025 ([NIST](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/61/r3/final)). Each such question should name the model it tests. A shared core deck of about 60 concepts tagged to all three exams, with per-exam overlays, fits the overlap in the official outlines. The research notes hold about 160 seed definitions and port numbers, but they were written from recall and need a spot-check against IANA and current study guides before shipping.

The content rules are strict. CompTIA states that people who use "brain dump" material "will have their certifications revoked and be suspended from future testing", and its objectives document prohibits reproduction without written consent ([SY0-801 Exam Objectives](https://lecbyo.files.cmp.optimizely.com/download/77f3bd3223ac11f180820e495f189928)). The app should therefore write every question originally against the public objectives, store objective numbers with paraphrased labels, refuse to ingest or reproduce recalled exam items, and carry a not-affiliated notice. Readiness thresholds are folklore: no vendor publishes one, and third-party advice ranges from 75% on unseen questions to 85% on five consecutive tests ([Infosec Institute](https://www.infosecinstitute.com/resources/professional-development/certification-test-anxiety/), [PrepForCerts](https://prepforcerts.org/comptia-security-plus-exam-tips)). Any in-app "ready" indicator should be labelled a heuristic.

## Small daily units and forgiveness retain better than ambitious goals

### What the evidence supports

Gamification helps learning modestly on average. Two 2020 meta-analyses put the effect near **g = 0.5 on cognitive outcomes** (g = .49 in Sailer and Homner, g = 0.504 in Bai and colleagues), with a weaker and less stable g = .36 for motivation ([Sailer & Homner](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10648-019-09498-w), [Bai et al.](https://www.gbl.uzh.ch/quartz/references/Bai-et-al.-(2020))). The documented risk is the reward itself. Deci, Koestner and Ryan's meta-analysis of 128 experiments found tangible rewards undermined intrinsic motivation, with effects of d = -0.28 to -0.40 across contingency types, while positive verbal feedback raised it by about d = 0.33 ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10589297/)). That conclusion is contested, and a later reanalysis locates the harm in rewards that are tangible, expected and loosely tied to performance ([Behavior Analyst](https://link.springer.com/doi/10.1007/BF03392017)). Points and levels raise the quantity of output without changing intrinsic motivation ([Mekler et al.](https://edoc-vmtest.ub.unibas.ch/44253/1/diss_emekler_ub_noCV.pdf)). In a study app, that is the grinding risk stated precisely: pay for volume and volume is what arrives. Game fiction was a significant moderator of behavioural outcomes, which supports investing in the sect narrative over bare points ([Sailer & Homner, Augsburg record](https://opus.bibliothek.uni-augsburg.de/opus4/frontdoor/index/index/docId/109056)).

Duolingo's published experiments give the clearest habit lever, with the caveat that they are company-reported and measure return visits, not learning. Letting a single lesson extend the streak, instead of requiring the full daily goal, raised **Day-14 retention by 3.3% and daily learners on a streak by 10.5%**; learners with the most intense daily goal were the least likely to hold a streak ([Duolingo, Improving the streak](https://blog.duolingo.com/improving-the-streak)). Allowing two streak freezes raised daily active learners by 0.38% "without encouraging more days off" ([Duolingo, How the streak builds habit](https://blog.duolingo.com/how-duolingo-streak-builds-habit)). Habitica shows both poles: missed dailies cost health, while "Rest in the Inn" pauses all damage and streak loss ([Habitica wiki](https://habitica.fandom.com/wiki/Rest_in_the_Inn)). Evidence for harm from streaks is thin, consisting of journalism, one qualitative paper on "gamification misuse" and a student thesis ([arXiv 2203.16175](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.16175)). The cautious reading is that loss-framed mechanics are unproven as harmful but unnecessary for a single-player app with no revenue to protect.

### Idle and cultivation structure, adapted to a bounded income

Idle games run on a few formulas: next cost equals `base * rate^owned`, with a rate of 1.07 in AdVenture Capitalist and 1.15 in Cookie Clicker ([Pecorella Part I](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/the-math-of-idle-games-part-i), [Cookie Clicker wiki](https://cookieclicker.wiki.gg/wiki/Buildings)). Egg, Inc. caps offline earnings at two hours specifically to push players toward active play ([Pecorella Part III](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/the-math-of-idle-games-part-iii)). These curves assume income compounds. A learner's income is bounded by 10-20 minutes of study a day, so commercial curves would stall the game in its second week. The adaptation is short ladders of five to ten levels per hall at 1.15, with the hard gates being mastery checks instead of currency. Prestige resets fit poorly because knowledge does not reset; ascending to the next track (A2, or the next certification) replaces them.

Cultivation fiction supplies a ready two-level progress structure. The first four realms are near-universal (Qi Condensation, Foundation Establishment, Core Formation, Nascent Soul), usually with sub-stages inside each ([Wuxiaworld](https://www.wuxiaworld.com/page/introduction-to-chinese-webnovel-genres-xianxia)). Amazing Cultivation Simulator adds the most useful mechanic: the quality of a Golden Core depends on what was accumulated before the breakthrough ([re-actor guide](https://re-actor.net/amazing-cultivation-simulator-golden-cores-for-dummies)). Mapped to study, sub-stages advance with ordinary reviews, a realm change requires a tribulation that is a mock exam or level test, and the score sets a modest permanent bonus.

### Proposed economy rules

No study compares reward schemes inside a spaced-repetition product, so everything in this subsection is design reasoning with starting values to playtest. The rules that follow from the evidence above are these. Only scheduled work pays in full, with off-schedule practice paying about a tenth. A self-graded "forgot" pays the same as a success, so honesty costs nothing, and accuracy bonuses come only from typed or auto-graded items. Returns diminish past the daily commitment, with no timed double-XP windows. The minimum unit that keeps the chain is clearing due reviews or five minutes; the 10-20 minute target is a stretch goal. The weekly goal is five days of seven, with automatic grace days and a manual "closed-door seclusion" mode. Passive spirit-vein income caps at 24 hours and stays a minority of total income. Absence lowers a temporary multiplier and never destroys buildings or disciples. A failed tribulation costs a short cooldown and produces a remediation list. Leaderboards are omitted, since they carry the clearest downside evidence and the app is single-player.

## Conclusion

The four research threads converge on one architecture. FSRS already computes, for every card, the probability the learner can recall it, and that number is the honest signal the game layer needs. Paying spirit stones per scheduled review and gating realms on test scores means the sect economy reads directly from the scheduler and the mock-exam engine, with no separate "engagement" metric to exploit. The same recall probability can sort a backlog, drive a "stabilise qi" recovery plan, and show the learner "items you can produce from memory" beside "items seen". The existing `cultivation.py` realm ladder and spirit-stone currency give that mapping a home in the current codebase.

The open risks are about content, not mechanics. Certification facts now have a shelf life of months, so exam codes, weights, retirement dates and incident-response models belong in versioned data with a review date, and the seed flashcards need verification before release. The Spanish inventory needs the official Cervantes source once it is reachable, and both language packs need a native review. The payout numbers and the 10-new-items default are untested proposals, and logging every review from day one is what will let them be tuned against real retention later.
